MONSTRUM

A single monstrous face, always new.

The machine invents a monster and never stops changing it: a face cut out of geometric shapes, like a collage. The scraps come from images of monsters found live in open archives — gorgons and mascarons, oni and taotie, gargoyles, hellmouths, bestiaries, woodcuts of prodigies, film stills — and from coloured paper. Eyes, horns, teeth, mouths, snouts, beaks and tentacles are reinvented at every moment: how many, where, of what shape; the eyes look around; now and then the whole monster is cut again. Its name is made from the syllables of the monsters it is cut from. The texts are encyclopedic descriptions of monsters, in several languages.

monstrum: from monere, to warn. Cicero heard in it monstrare, to show.

A project by Jacopo Natoli, 2026
