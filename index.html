MOSTRAI

A single monstrous face, always new.

Its genome mixes a bestiary: strange, monstrous and ordinary animals (anglerfish, bats, mandrills, star-nosed moles, axolotls, spiders, mantises, octopuses, lampreys), plants and fungi (flytraps, rafflesia, lotus pods, cordyceps, mandrakes), and sculpture (Brancusi, Moore, Calder, Giacometti, Fontana). Sometimes it turns into pure geometry. It insists on what makes a face frightening: eyes with too much white or none at all, sunken sockets, mouths cut too wide, jaws hanging too long, faces too long, features sliding or upside down, stillness and then a snap. Other voices enter the flow: Francis Bacon, Giulia Cenci, vampires and stitched creatures of novels, films and comics, frightening clowns, strange children, the man with the growths, the devils of medieval frescoes, gargoyles, the demons of anime, zombies; masculine, feminine and queer signs worn together; the opened body of Monique Wittig's Le corps lesbien. Tongues, teeth, bones, gills, wounds, mucus, smoke. Sometimes the monster is only lines. Then Ulisse Aldrovandi's woodcuts of monsters, with their hatching; Paul B. Preciado's monster that speaks from its cage; the threat faces of animals — wrinkled muzzles, lips pulled over the canines, ears pinned back, bristling fur, the open gape. At times the face stays still and only the mouth and its tongues move; at times almost everything is taken away. It watches you.

The machine invents a monster and never stops changing it: a face made of geometric shapes in the three primary colours, white and black, on a ground that changes colour too. It mixes the grammar of many monsters without copying any: Mesoamerican and Andean masks, Amazonian feather crowns, Caribbean carnival, the demon faces of India, Sri Lanka and Bali, Himalayan masks, oni and yokai, anime, taotie and opera faces, African and Oceanic masks, gorgons, medieval grotesques, commedia and carnival, skulls, cartoons, robots, aliens, insects, sea creatures, cubism, Miró, the Bauhaus, pixels, the first idols. Eyes, horns, crowns, teeth, tongues, beaks, ears and tentacles are reinvented at every moment, while the face breathes, blinks, looks around, chews and sways, at ever-changing speeds and rhythms.

monstrum: from monere, to warn. Cicero heard in it monstrare, to show.

A project by Jacopo Natoli, 2026
