MONSTRUM

A single monstrous face, endlessly metamorphic.

Every few seconds a new monster is invented. Its flesh comes from images of monsters found live in open archives — gorgons and mascarons, oni and taotie, gargoyles, hellmouths, bestiaries, woodcuts of prodigies, film stills — and, when available, from creatures generated by an AI. In every image a machine eye looks for the most symmetrical region, the place where a face could be. Then an anatomy engine reinvents it: it mirrors and folds it, joins the eyes into one or multiplies them in rings and rows, puts eyes where the mouth was, swells, twists and melts the flesh. Brow, eyes and mouth come from different monsters; each monster turns into the next and the face never settles. Its name is made each time from the syllables of its sources. The texts are encyclopedic descriptions of monsters, in several languages.

monstrum: from monere, to warn. Cicero heard in it monstrare, to show.
